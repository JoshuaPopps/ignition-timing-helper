Your build

Before you bolt it in

  • Gold rotor only — HD part no. 32402-83. The earlier silver cup will not work properly.
  • Coil primary must be 2.5–3.5 ohms. A coil outside that range can fault the module (rapid-flash red LED).
  • Suppression plug wires only — carbon, graphite, or spiral core. No metal-core wires.
  • Green wire is the tach output. Do not connect it to the coil.
  • Power cycle after changing switches — new settings only take effect when power goes off and back on.

Flywheel mark math

Timing marks are just geometry: so many degrees around a wheel of a given diameter. Measure your own flywheel diameter across the marks and the tool does the arithmetic — no spec to look up, no number invented.

Degrees → distance between marks

Distance → degrees

Measured the gap between two marks with a tape? Work out what it means in degrees.

Factory reference, for a stock Evo: the advance mark sits 20° BTDC from the TDC mark (#99482-98). On this calculator, 20° on an 8.5 in flywheel is about 1.48 in — but measure your own wheel; flywheel diameters vary by year and model, and the tool does not assume one.

Bench tests

Measure it, type the number in, get a pass or fail against the published spec. No guessing — the spec is shown next to your reading.

Coil resistance — factory spec

Primary 2.5–3.1 Ω; secondary 10,000–12,500 Ω (#99482-98). Measure with the coil disconnected, meter on the right range. Leave a side blank if you did not measure it.

V.O.E.S. vacuum test — factory spec

The switch must read open at rest, and close (<1 Ω) at 3.5–4.5 inHg vacuum (89–114 mm Hg) — vacuum pump HD-23738 (#99482-98, Ch. 8).

How this tool works — and what it isn't

What it is

A garage-side timing helper for carbureted Evo Harleys: the factory static and dynamic procedures, the Dyna 2000i mode switches, flywheel mark math, and the coil / V.O.E.S. bench tests — with a log so the next time you time a bike you know what you did last time.

Where the numbers come from

  • Factory procedures and specs — Harley-Davidson 1997–1998 Softail Service Manual #99482-98, Chapter 1 ("Ignition Timing and V.O.E.S.") and Chapter 8 ("Ignition Timing", "V.O.E.S."). Timing setting 20° BTDC at 1050–1500 rpm, check every 5000 miles.
  • Dyna 2000i curves, switches, coils, and LEDs — Dynatek Dyna 2000i-P instructions, part 2801103 Rev. C (the manufacturer's instruction sheet).
  • One note from the earlier sheet — the 1995-and-later flywheel mark warning comes from the Dynatek Dyna 2000 instructions, Rev. 2/99, and is labeled as such.

What it isn't

  • Not a service manual. If a step here disagrees with your manual, your manual wins — this tool is a quick reference, not a replacement.
  • Not a dyno or a tuning guide. Jetting lives in the carb journal; this tool only times the spark.
  • It never invents a spec. Curves 5–8 on the 2000i are programmable-only and the sheet gives no switch positions for them, so the configurator refuses them instead of guessing. Same for any model year or ignition this tool has no published data for.

Privacy

Everything runs on your device — plain JavaScript, no server, no network, no account. The log lives in this browser's local storage and moves only as a JSON file you export yourself. Works offline from the first visit; add it to your home screen and it lives there.
